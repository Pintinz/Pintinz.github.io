Graduate trainee interviews follow a fairly predictable pattern: questions about you, your motivation for the role and company, and examples of your skills. Preparing for the common questions lets you spend your energy on the unexpected ones.

## Questions about you

**1. Tell me about yourself.**
Give a 60-second summary: what you studied, one or two relevant experiences, and why you're interested in this role now. Finish by linking back to the job.

**2. What are your strengths?**
Choose two strengths the role needs and give a short example of each.

**3. What is your biggest weakness?**
Pick a real, non-critical weakness and explain what you're doing to improve it. Avoid disguised strengths like "I work too hard".

**4. Where do you see yourself in five years?**
Show ambition that fits the programme: building expertise, taking on responsibility and growing within the company.

## Questions about motivation

**5. Why do you want to join this programme?**
Mention specific features such as the rotations, training, sector or values. Generic answers that could apply to any employer are a red flag.

**6. What do you know about our company?**
Research the company's products, markets, recent news and values before the interview, and mention one or two things that genuinely interest you.

**7. Why should we hire you?**
Summarise the match between what they need and what you offer, in three points.

## Competency questions

Use the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for these. See our [STAR method guide](star-method-interview-answers.html).

**8. Tell me about a time you worked in a team.**

**9. Describe a time you solved a difficult problem.**

**10. Give an example of when you showed leadership.**

**11. Tell me about a time you failed or made a mistake.**

**12. Describe a situation where you had to meet a tight deadline.**

## Situational and closing questions

**13. What would you do if you disagreed with your manager?**
Show that you'd raise concerns respectfully, with evidence, and then support the final decision.

**14. How do you handle pressure?**
Describe your methods (planning, prioritising, asking for help early) and back them with a brief example.

**15. Do you have any questions for us?**
Always ask one or two. Good options: what a typical first year looks like, how trainees are supported, or what success looks like at the end of the programme.

## Before the interview

- Re-read the job description and match each requirement to one of your examples.
- Prepare your STAR stories and practise them out loud.
- Check the format (video, phone, in person or assessment centre) and test your setup if it's online.
