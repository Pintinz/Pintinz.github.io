The 2026 NNPC/SNEPCo National University Scholarship is run by Shell Nigeria Exploration and Production Company (SNEPCo) for itself, NNPC Limited and its co-venture partners. It pays a yearly grant from your second year until you finish your degree. Applications close on **30 November 2026**, and the next hurdle is a computer-based test. This guide covers what's officially known and how to prepare.

> CareerOS is not affiliated with NNPC, SNEPCo or Scholastica. Every fact about the scheme below comes from the official advert on Scholastica. Confirm details there before you apply.

## Who can apply

The official advert lists four requirements. You must:

- be a Nigerian citizen;
- be in your **second year** of full-time study at a **public** Nigerian university, on an NUC-accredited course;
- have a CGPA of at least **3.5 on a 5-point scale** (or the equivalent);
- not already hold a scholarship from another oil and gas company, Nigerian or international.

Female candidates are strongly encouraged to apply. Applications go through the Scholastica portal only. Email applications are not accepted, and the application is **free**. SNEPCo says it doesn't use agents, so ignore anyone who asks you to pay to "process" your application.

## How selection works

According to the official advert:

1. Only candidates who meet the eligibility criteria are invited to a **proctored (monitored) computer-based test**.
2. Exam details are sent to shortlisted candidates only, so watch the email address you applied with, including the spam folder.
3. The **top 10 candidates from each of the 36 states and the FCT** are shortlisted for academic verification before any award.
4. Scholars must keep a CGPA of at least 3.5 every session to keep the award.

That means you're competing with applicants from your own state, and only ten move on. Your test score decides whether you get through, so preparation matters.

## What the test is likely to cover

SNEPCo doesn't publish a syllabus for the test. Scholarship and graduate computer-based tests in Nigeria commonly include:

- **Numerical reasoning**: percentages, ratios, speed and time, and reading tables.
- **Verbal reasoning**: reading a short passage and judging whether statements are true, false or can't be determined.
- **Logical reasoning**: number sequences, patterns and simple deductions.

Treat this as a sensible way to prepare, not a guarantee of the format. If your invitation email names the sections, practise those first.

## Practice questions

These are original CareerOS practice questions written in the style of a scholarship CBT. They are **not** past questions from the real test.

**1. Percentages.** A student's monthly allowance rises from ₦40,000 to ₦46,000. What is the percentage increase?

*Answer: 15%.* The increase is ₦6,000, and 6,000 ÷ 40,000 = 0.15.

**2. Speed and time.** A bus travels 180 km in 2 hours 30 minutes. What is its average speed?

*Answer: 72 km/h.* 2 hours 30 minutes is 2.5 hours, and 180 ÷ 2.5 = 72.

**3. Ratios.** A class of 64 students has boys and girls in the ratio 3 : 5. How many girls are there?

*Answer: 40.* There are 3 + 5 = 8 parts, so each part is 64 ÷ 8 = 8 students, and girls are 5 × 8 = 40.

**4. Number sequence.** What comes next: 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ?

*Answer: 42.* The gaps grow by 2 each time (4, 6, 8, 10), so the next gap is 12.

**5. Verbal reasoning.** Read the passage, then decide whether each statement is true, false or can't say.

*"The award is open to second-year students in public universities. Applicants must have a CGPA of at least 3.5, and scholars must keep that CGPA every session."*

- Statement A: "A third-year student with a CGPA of 4.2 can apply." *False*: the award is for second-year students.
- Statement B: "Most applicants study engineering." *Can't say*: the passage doesn't mention courses.
- Statement C: "A scholar whose CGPA drops to 3.2 may lose the award." *True*: scholars must keep a CGPA of at least 3.5.

**6. Logical deduction.** All interns attend the safety briefing. Some people at the safety briefing are engineers. Does it follow that some interns are engineers?

*Answer: No, it can't be concluded.* The engineers at the briefing might not be interns at all.

## A two-week preparation plan

Shortlisted candidates often get only a short notice period, so start before the invitation arrives.

- **Days 1–2:** take one timed practice set in each area to find your weakest section.
- **Days 3–7:** spend most of your time on that weakest section. Learn the method, then do short timed sets.
- **Days 8–11:** mix all sections in timed sets that match the time you're likely to get, about a minute per question.
- **Days 12–13:** take two full mock tests under exam conditions, with no phone and no breaks.
- **Day 14:** rest, check your invitation details and prepare what you need to bring.

## On the day

- Read the invitation carefully: the date, time, venue or online link, and the ID you must bring.
- Arrive early, or log in early for an online test, so a late start doesn't eat your time.
- Keep an eye on the clock. If a question takes twice your average time, choose your best answer and move on, unless the instructions say wrong answers are penalised.
- Make sure the names on your documents match your application. The advert says mismatched names need an affidavit or certificate.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is the 2026 NNPC/SNEPCo scholarship test?

SNEPCo hasn't published a date. The advert says exam details will be communicated to shortlisted candidates only, so check the email you applied with, including spam.

### Is the NNPC/SNEPCo scholarship free to apply for?

Yes. The official advert says the application is free and SNEPCo doesn't assign agents or representatives. Never pay anyone to apply on your behalf.

### Are there official NNPC/SNEPCo past questions?

SNEPCo doesn't publish past questions. Be wary of anyone selling "leaked" or "official" past questions. Practising the common test areas is the reliable way to prepare.

### Can private university students apply?

No. The 2026 advert is for students at public Nigerian universities on NUC-accredited courses.

### What CGPA do I need?

At least 3.5 on a 5-point scale (or the equivalent) to apply, and you must keep 3.5 every session to keep the award.
