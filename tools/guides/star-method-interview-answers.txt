Questions that start with "Tell me about a time when…" or "Give an example of…" are competency questions. The interviewer wants evidence that you've already shown a skill they need, such as teamwork, problem-solving or leadership. The STAR method is the simplest way to structure that evidence.

## What STAR stands for

- **Situation**: the background. Where were you and what was happening? Keep it to one or two sentences.
- **Task**: what you were responsible for, or the goal.
- **Action**: what *you* did, step by step. This should be the longest part.
- **Result**: what happened because of your actions, ideally with a number, and what you learned.

## A worked example

**Question:** Tell me about a time you worked under pressure.

- **Situation:** "In my final year, two weeks before our group project was due, one of our four members left the course."
- **Task:** "As the person managing the timeline, I had to make sure we still submitted a complete project on time."
- **Action:** "I split the missing work into smaller tasks, matched them to each person's strengths, and set up short check-ins every two days. I took on the data analysis myself because it was the biggest gap."
- **Result:** "We submitted a day early and received one of the highest marks in the class. I learned to break a big problem into small, visible pieces."

## Common mistakes

- **Saying "we" the whole time.** The interviewer is hiring you. Say "I" for your own actions.
- **Too much Situation, too little Action.** Aim for roughly 20% Situation and Task, 60% Action, 20% Result.
- **No result.** Even if things didn't go perfectly, explain the outcome and what you'd do differently.
- **Made-up stories.** Interviewers ask follow-up questions. Use real examples from study, work, volunteering or personal projects.

## Prepare 6 to 8 stories in advance

Most competency questions draw on a small set of skills. Prepare one real example for each and you can adapt them to almost any question:

1. Working in a team
2. Leading or influencing others
3. Solving a difficult problem
4. Handling pressure or a tight deadline
5. Dealing with a mistake or failure
6. Going beyond what was expected
7. Handling conflict or a difficult person
8. Learning something new quickly

## Practise out loud

A STAR answer should take about 90 seconds to 2 minutes. Practise saying your stories out loud and time yourself. Writing them down helps you remember them, but speaking them is what makes them sound natural.
